Late detection is the loss

By the time a pest or disease outbreak is visible across a block, most of the yield decision has already been made. The prototype's job is to shorten the gap between the first signal in a field and the first action taken on it.

What we see today

A leaf photo, three low-cost sensors and local weather context. Each on its own is weak evidence; fused, they carry a usable early signal.

Where quantum enters

The fused agricultural features are encoded onto four qubits and classified by a variational quantum circuit — run beside a classical baseline on identical inputs, never instead of it. The exact encoding depends on which engine is active; the Quantum Lab names it precisely.

Who acts on it

Farmers get one plain next step. Field officers, RBKs, departments and insurers get the evidence trail, confidence and history behind it.

Prototype status

Functional proof-of-concept

The prototype demonstrates an end-to-end agricultural early-warning workflow combining crop imagery, field and context data, classical feature processing and an experimental Qiskit-based QML layer. Real-world multispectral deployment, large-scale field validation and practical quantum advantage remain future validation stages.

No quantum advantage is claimed. Benchmarks run on a labelled demonstration dataset and the circuit executes on a simulator, never on quantum hardware.

Judge's 60 seconds

Problem. Crop disease and pest outbreaks are detected too late.
Solution. Fuse crop imagery, sensors and weather into an early-warning signal.
Quantum. A 4-qubit variational classifier over that multimodal feature space, in Qiskit.
Impact. Targeted, earlier intervention for farmers, RBKs, departments and insurers.
Scale. Crops, states, sensors and remote-sensing sources are additive inputs, not rewrites.

Quantum intelligence

What Qiskit actually does here

Eight multimodal agricultural features are ranked, the strongest four are encoded onto four qubits, and a variational circuit trained by a classical optimiser produces an outbreak-risk probability. The circuit and the exact encoding shown below are read back from the model that actually runs — never a static diagram.
